Tooltip Guidance

Tooltip guidance provides brief, noninteractive supplemental information for an already understandable trigger. It improves context without becoming the trigger's accessible name, an essential instruction, or a container for additional interaction.

Composition boundary

Tooltip Guidance owns when supplemental description is appropriate, not the Tooltip component's mechanics. Tooltip owns the anchor relationship, `aria-describedby`, `role="tooltip"`, supported placement, visibility behavior, pointer/focus interaction, and Escape dismissal. The trigger retains its own semantic role and accessible name.

Popover owns richer or interactive disclosure. Persistent inline help owns information users need in order to understand or complete the task. Do not move required instructions into a Tooltip merely to reduce visible content.

When a tooltip is appropriate

  • Supplement an already understandable trigger. Tooltip text may clarify context, terminology, timing, or a nonessential detail, but the trigger must remain understandable without it.
  • Do not use Tooltip as the accessible name. Icon-only triggers need an independent accessible name such as `aria-label`; the Tooltip description supplements that name through `aria-describedby`.
  • Keep essential instructions persistent. Requirements, validation rules, safety information, and steps necessary to complete a task should remain visible or otherwise persistently available.
  • Use Popover for richer disclosure. If the content needs headings, links, buttons, form controls, or substantially more explanation, it has crossed the Tooltip boundary.
  • Do not add tooltips merely to explain familiar controls. Supplemental disclosure should solve an information need rather than compensate for unclear labels or unnecessary icon-only design.

Content & placement

  • Keep content brief and singular in purpose. A Tooltip should normally communicate one compact idea rather than a paragraph, checklist, or mini help article.
  • Use plain text, not interactive content. The Tooltip itself is not focusable and must not contain links, buttons, or other controls.
  • Use supported placement intentionally. Concord supports top and bottom placement. Choose the position that keeps the Tooltip readable without promising unsupported collision or anchoring behavior.
  • Allow constrained content to wrap. The canonical Tooltip owns a maximum width and resilient wrapping; do not force one-line content when localization or longer terminology needs more space.
  • Do not treat placement as meaning. The relationship comes from semantics and proximity; top versus bottom must not change what the information means.

Interaction & dismissal

  • Reveal the same guidance for pointer hover and keyboard focus. Do not provide information to mouse users that keyboard users cannot obtain.
  • Keep focus on the trigger. The Tooltip is descriptive, not a focus destination. Opening or dismissing it must not move keyboard focus into the Tooltip.
  • Keep hover-triggered content reachable by the pointer. Users must be able to move from the trigger onto the visible Tooltip without it disappearing immediately.
  • Allow Escape to dismiss enhanced Tooltips. Dismissal should hide the Tooltip while leaving focus on its trigger.
  • Do not require activation to read essential content. Hover is unavailable on many touch interactions and Tooltip activation behavior may vary by input mode; required information must not depend on Tooltip access.

Trigger & accessibility guidance

  • Use an appropriate already-named control as the trigger. A native button is appropriate for Concord's icon-trigger Tooltip when it owns focus and has an independent accessible name.
  • Do not rely on a disabled native control as the only trigger. Disabled controls may not receive focus or pointer events consistently. If users need to know why an action is unavailable, provide that explanation through persistent workflow guidance rather than a Tooltip-only dependency.
  • Preserve the descriptive relationship. The trigger references the Tooltip through `aria-describedby`; the Tooltip uses `role="tooltip"`.
  • Do not duplicate the accessible name in the Tooltip merely to create a visual label. Supplemental text should add useful context rather than cause repetitive announcements.
  • Use the canonical enhancement rather than pattern-specific scripting. Tooltip behavior belongs to the component contract; Patterns should not fork hover, focus, Escape, or visibility logic.

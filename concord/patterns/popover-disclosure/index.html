Patterns / Disclosure

Popover Disclosure

Popover disclosure reveals structured, potentially interactive contextual content while leaving the surrounding interface available. It is appropriate when a Tooltip is too limited but temporarily blocking the page with a Modal would be unnecessary.

Composition boundary

Popover Disclosure owns the contextual relationship and disclosure decision, not the Popover component mechanics. Popover owns the native invoker relationship, top-layer surface, anchor-relative placement, viewport containment, explicit close anatomy, and native auto-popover dismissal behavior. The consuming application owns any product action, saved state, or data change performed by controls inside the surface.

Tooltip owns brief noninteractive supplemental description. Menu owns a command-oriented composite widget with menu-item semantics and arrow-key navigation. Modal owns focused tasks that must make the surrounding page temporarily unavailable. Persistent inline disclosure remains preferable when users need the information continuously rather than temporarily.

When a popover is appropriate

  • Use Popover for contextual structured disclosure. It fits information or controls that belong to a specific trigger and need more structure or interaction than a Tooltip.
  • Use Tooltip for one brief noninteractive idea. Do not promote simple descriptive text into a Popover merely to add a larger surface.
  • Use Menu for commands, not general content. A list of application actions that needs menu semantics, arrow-key movement, and menu-item behavior belongs to Menu rather than a generic Popover.
  • Use Modal when background interaction must stop. Popover remains non-modal; it must not become an improvised focus-trapping dialog for work that actually requires modality.
  • Prefer inline disclosure when content must remain available. Instructions or information users need continuously should not disappear through light dismiss.

Structure & semantics

  • Use a real invoker. The canonical trigger is a native Button with `popovertarget`; that relationship also supplies the anchor used by Concord's current placement contract.
  • Give structured Popovers a visible programmatic label. Concord's canonical structured surface uses `role="dialog"` with `aria-labelledby` pointing to its visible heading.
  • Keep an explicit close path. The canonical close Button targets the same Popover with `popovertargetaction="hide"` and has an accessible name that identifies what it closes.
  • Keep interactive content in ordinary document semantics. Form controls, links, and actions inside a Popover retain their own native/component contracts; Popover does not redefine their keyboard behavior.
  • Do not add Menu semantics to general disclosure. A Popover containing ordinary controls remains structured dialog-like disclosure, not a `role="menu"` composite.

Focus & dismissal

  • Keep the interaction non-modal. Opening a Popover does not make the background inert and does not establish a focus trap.
  • Preserve normal focus order. Interactive descendants remain keyboard reachable through ordinary Tab navigation. Do not add roving focus or arrow-key behavior unless a nested component independently owns that contract.
  • Support an explicit close control even when light dismiss exists. Outside activation and Escape are convenient native dismissal paths, but they should not be the only understandable way to close richer disclosure.
  • Let native auto-popover behavior own Escape and light dismiss. Do not duplicate browser dismissal with Pattern-specific document listeners or an artificial backdrop.
  • Keep dismissal non-destructive. Closing the surface should normally abandon only the disclosure itself. If dismissing would discard meaningful unsaved work or requires a decision, reconsider whether the interaction should be Modal or a persistent workflow.

Placement & resilience

  • Treat side and alignment as presentation, not meaning. The canonical Popover may prefer a side/alignment and use anchor-positioning fallbacks when space is constrained. Content meaning must not depend on appearing above, below, left, or right.
  • Keep the surface inside the available viewport. Concord's component constrains the Popover and lets its content region scroll when necessary rather than allowing the disclosure to become unreachable.
  • Allow content to wrap and localize. Headings, supporting copy, and nested control labels must remain readable at narrow widths and with longer translated text.
  • Keep heading and close access stable during scrolling. Long disclosure content should scroll within the bounded content region while the surrounding Popover structure remains usable.
  • Do not add Pattern-level positioning JavaScript. Placement and collision behavior belong to the canonical Popover implementation and browser platform contract.

Actions & state

  • Keep actions proportional to the disclosure. Popovers can contain controls and local actions, but a large multi-step task or consequential decision usually belongs in a page flow or Modal.
  • Preserve underlying component ownership. Checkbox, Button, Link, and other controls inside the surface retain their canonical semantics, states, and accessibility behavior.
  • Do not imply persistence from presentation alone. The consuming application decides whether a changed control applies immediately, requires Save/Cancel, can fail, or needs Status Messaging afterward.
  • Avoid hiding unresolved requirements through light dismiss. If users must act on information before continuing, the workflow should not depend on a dismissible non-modal surface.

Multi-step Flow

Multi-step Flow demonstrates how a known ordered sequence, page-level form content, progress communication, validation, and navigation actions work together without turning Progress Steps into decoration or the Template into an application state machine.

Sequence contract

Progress communicates state; the application owns the workflow

Concord composes completed, current, and future step semantics with the current page content. It does not calculate routing, branching, persistence, or completion.

Completed step A prior step the application has accepted. Completion does not make the step navigable.
Current step Exactly one active step. Progress Steps exposes it with aria-current="step".
Future step Known later steps remain visible without becoming active controls or destinations.
Workflow complete After final submission, communicate the outcome instead of leaving the last editable step current.

Composition boundary

Multi-step Flow owns the page-level relationship among Progress Steps, the current step heading and content, validation and correction, Back/Continue actions, and completion communication. It does not own the internal contracts of Progress Steps, Input, Select, Radio, Button, Form Layout, Form Validation, Action Groups, Confirmation, Loading & Progress, or Status Messaging.

The consuming application owns routing, step data, persistence, authorization, branching rules, dirty state, save/resume behavior, server validation, completion state, and whether previously completed steps can be revisited.

Do not introduce a `dsl-multi-step-flow` component, a Template router, or a Template persistence API merely to package this composition.

Known sequence & progress meaning

  • Use Multi-step Flow only when the user is intentionally moving through a known ordered sequence whose steps have meaningful identity.
  • Progress Steps communicates where the user is in that sequence; it must not be decorative.
  • Mark completed steps as completed, exactly one active step as current, and later steps as inactive or future.
  • Keep the visible step labels aligned with the actual workflow. Do not rename steps solely to make the Progress Steps component fit a preferred width.
  • Do not imply percentage completion when the workflow is not meaningfully proportional.
  • If the total sequence can change because of branching, update the communicated sequence deliberately rather than leaving stale progress.
  • A single long form with validation is Form Flow, not Multi-step Flow.

Current step composition

  • Give the current step one clear heading and concise explanation before its fields or content.
  • Keep field labels, descriptions, errors, and groups in the canonical Form Layout and Form Validation relationships.
  • Preserve values already entered in the current step when validation fails.
  • Keep the current step content bounded to a useful reading and interaction measure.
  • Do not duplicate the Progress Steps label as a second competing page title.
  • Content unique to one step belongs in the step region; persistent sequence-level context belongs outside it.

Back, Continue & step navigation

  • Back returns to the prior meaningful step without masquerading as form submission.
  • Continue validates or submits only the current step boundary required by the application.
  • Keep Back and Continue distinct from Cancel, Save for later, or final Submit actions.
  • Preserve user-entered values when moving backward unless the product deliberately resets them.
  • Progress Steps is not automatically navigation. Do not make completed markers clickable unless revisiting those steps is genuinely supported and the interaction has an accessible destination contract.
  • When direct step navigation is supported, routing and unsaved-change policy remain application-owned.

Validation across steps

  • Validate information at the step where the user can understand and correct it.
  • Keep field-level errors adjacent to the affected control or group.
  • Do not force the user to discover an earlier-step error only at final submission when it could reasonably have been resolved earlier.
  • Server validation may invalidate data entered on a previous step; when that occurs, return the user to an understandable correction path and preserve unrelated work.
  • Do not mark future steps invalid before the user reaches them.
  • Error summaries, when warranted, supplement rather than replace field-level correction.

Completion & final submission

  • Distinguish moving to the next step from the final consequential submission.
  • The final step may use Confirmation when the user must deliberately review or approve a consequential action.
  • After final submission succeeds, communicate a clear completion outcome through Status Messaging or a new destination.
  • Do not leave the final editable step looking current after the workflow has completed.
  • On recoverable final-submission failure, preserve the review context and provide a clear retry or correction path.
  • Completion state remains application-owned; the Template describes how it is composed and communicated.

Save, resume & persistence

  • Save-for-later is optional product behavior, not a requirement of Multi-step Flow.
  • If save/resume exists, communicate whether current-step changes are already persisted or still pending.
  • Do not imply that moving Back or Continue persists data unless the application actually does so.
  • Restore the user to a meaningful step when resuming, and keep Progress Steps synchronized with restored state.
  • Persistence, expiry, conflict resolution, and cross-device continuity remain application-owned.

Branching & conditional steps

  • Use branching only when the sequence genuinely changes based on earlier information or application rules.
  • If a step is added or removed, update both the visible Progress Steps and the current step relationship.
  • Do not expose inaccessible future branches merely to show every possible step.
  • Preserve meaningful history when a user changes an earlier answer that alters later steps.
  • Applications own the branch graph and data dependencies; Concord owns only the presentation contracts used to communicate the resulting sequence.

Focus, history & announcements

  • Keep keyboard order aligned with semantic source order: progress context, current step content, then step actions.
  • When moving to another step in place, move focus deliberately to the new step heading or another useful recovery target according to the application's navigation model.
  • Full-document navigation may rely on normal browser focus and history behavior.
  • Keep browser Back/Forward behavior meaningful when steps are represented by navigable locations.
  • Announce meaningful asynchronous step changes politely when needed; do not make the entire Template a live region.
  • Do not move focus merely because Progress Steps changes visual state.

Responsive composition & content resilience

  • Preserve semantic step order and current-step content order at narrow widths.
  • Let Progress Steps use its canonical responsive/compact behavior rather than inventing a second mobile progress control.
  • Allow long translated step names, labels, validation messages, and generated content to wrap without horizontal page overflow.
  • Stack Back/Continue actions when necessary without reversing their DOM order or semantic meaning.
  • Keep the current step readable at text zoom and with fallback fonts.
  • Do not hide progress entirely on narrow screens unless the product supplies an equally understandable sequence context.

Application ownership & state integrity

  • Current-step identity remains application-owned.
  • Step data and validation truth remain application-owned.
  • Routing, browser history, authorization, branching, persistence, save/resume, dirty state, and completion remain application-owned.
  • Concord Progress Steps communicates completed/current/future state; it does not calculate workflow state.
  • Concord Patterns own reusable form, validation, action, confirmation, loading, and status relationships.
  • The Template describes their page-scale composition without implementing the workflow engine.

Theme, density & page-scale validation

Validate the same semantic sequence across supported themes, light and dark appearance, and density settings. Theme and density may change spacing, surface expression, and control compactness, but they must not change step order, current-step meaning, source order, action hierarchy, validation semantics, or application ownership.

Content resilience guidance

Build content that remains readable when language, length, viewport, and presentation conditions change.

Reference guidance Updated September 2026 7 min read
On this page

  • Preserve document structure

Design for real content

Content rarely stays as short or predictable as an initial mockup. Interfaces need enough structural resilience for translated text, longer labels, editorial changes, and user-generated information.

Start with a readable content measure, then allow the page around it to use the available space. The article should remain understandable without depending on a wide desktop canvas.

  • Let paragraphs and labels wrap naturally.
  • Avoid fixed heights for text-owned regions.
  • Keep supporting information close to the content it explains.

Preserve document structure

Headings describe the document outline rather than a visual size scale. Supporting navigation should point to real section destinations, and responsive layout should not change the source order required to understand the article.

Content rule

Meaning comes from semantic HTML first. Layout may change where a region appears, but it should not replace or reorder the document contract.

Keep actions in context

Links should represent destinations and remain understandable when read outside the surrounding paragraph. Actions that change state should use the appropriate control instead of borrowing link styling.

Content contract

Readable content keeps meaning independent from placement

The Template owns page-scale hierarchy and composition. Native semantics define the document; the content platform still owns the records, routes, localization source, permissions, and publishing state.

Readable measure Long-form prose stays constrained while the surrounding page uses available width.
Semantic outline Heading levels and landmarks describe meaning rather than presentation size.
Supporting content Related guidance stays secondary and may move below the article when space narrows.
Content ownership Routes, records, localization, permissions, and publishing remain application-owned.

Composition boundary

Content Page owns the page-level relationship among the content header, readable article measure, semantic sections, supporting navigation, related content, and optional page-level actions.

Native HTML owns document meaning. Concord Layout owns spatial relationships and useful content boundaries. Existing components such as Link, Breadcrumbs, Media, or Button may participate when the content requires them, but the Template does not redefine their contracts.

The consuming application or content platform owns routes, permissions, content data, localization source, revision history, publishing workflow, analytics, and persistence.

Do not introduce a `dsl-content-page` component, a Template-specific rich-text API, or a fictional CMS contract merely to package this composition.

Readable measure & reading rhythm

  • Constrain long-form prose to a useful reading measure rather than stretching paragraphs across every available column.
  • Treat the readable measure as a maximum, not a fixed width. Narrow containers should use the available inline size without horizontal scrolling.
  • Keep headings, paragraphs, lists, quotations, figures, and related actions inside understandable local rhythm.
  • Use larger region spacing between major sections and smaller Flow relationships inside each section.
  • Avoid using line breaks in authored prose to force a visual measure.
  • Density may tune surrounding spacing, but it should not make long-form text cramped or materially change reading order.

Semantic hierarchy & landmarks

  • Use one meaningful page or article title and descend through heading levels according to document structure.
  • Do not choose heading levels for visual size. Styling may change without changing the semantic outline.
  • Use native `article`, `section`, `nav`, `aside`, `figure`, and list semantics when their meanings apply.
  • Give a section an accessible name when it is intended to function as a named region.
  • Keep repeated landmark types distinguishable when more than one of the same type appears on the page.
  • Preserve meaningful source order before applying side rails, multi-column arrangements, or responsive placement.

Supporting navigation & anchors

  • On-page navigation should link to stable section destinations that exist in the document.
  • Keep link labels aligned with the visible section headings they target.
  • Do not create a second page-navigation model when the content is short enough to scan without one.
  • When sticky presentation is used by a consuming product, ensure zoom and short viewports do not trap or obscure navigation.
  • Anchor navigation should preserve browser history and normal link behavior rather than being reimplemented as button state.
  • If a section is removed or renamed, update its navigation destination in the same content change.

Links & inline actions

  • Use links for destinations and Buttons for actions. Do not style one semantic role to masquerade as the other.
  • Keep inline links distinguishable from surrounding prose without relying on color alone.
  • Use concise link text that still makes sense when encountered out of paragraph context.
  • Reserve page-level actions for meaningful content tasks such as downloading, sharing, editing, or starting a related workflow.
  • Avoid turning every paragraph into a field of competing calls to action.
  • Related actions belong near the content whose next step they represent.

Supporting regions & related content

  • Supporting content should remain genuinely secondary to the article's primary reading path.
  • Related guidance, metadata, references, or contextual actions may occupy an aside when they are useful but not required to understand the main content.
  • Do not place essential instructions exclusively in a side region that may move below the article at narrow widths.
  • Keep supporting-region headings descriptive so the relationship remains understandable after reflow.
  • Preserve the same source order when the visual relationship changes.
  • A side rail is a spatial choice, not a new semantic hierarchy.

Long content, localization & generated content

  • Allow translated headings, long link labels, and user- or CMS-authored content to wrap without clipping or page overflow.
  • Do not rely on fixed block heights for content sections.
  • Preserve readable relationships when a paragraph, list item, or related-link label becomes substantially longer than the English example.
  • Long unbroken strings should wrap where the content type permits; code, identifiers, and URLs may need specialized overflow behavior.
  • Content insertion must not silently skip heading levels or create duplicate landmark names.
  • Generated content should preserve the same semantic contracts as authored content.

Media, figures & embedded content

  • Keep figures and Media within the available content boundary unless a documented page composition deliberately allows a wider presentation.
  • Provide captions when they add information that is not already evident from the surrounding prose.
  • Alternative text, captions, transcripts, and controls remain responsibilities of the participating media contract.
  • Do not make an image wider merely because the article measure is constrained.
  • Embedded interactive content must remain keyboard reachable, named, and resilient at zoom.

Accessibility, focus & reading order

  • Preserve a logical document outline and landmark structure that can be understood without the visual layout.
  • Keep keyboard order aligned with source order.
  • Do not move focus when an in-page anchor is activated unless the product has a deliberate focus-management requirement beyond normal browser behavior.
  • Support text zoom, browser zoom, fallback fonts, forced colors where applicable, and long localized content.
  • Ensure link purpose, heading names, and supporting-region labels remain understandable out of visual context.
  • Do not make the entire article a live region when content updates asynchronously.

Responsive composition & content resilience

  • Let the article keep its useful measure while the surrounding page uses available width.
  • Move supporting regions below the main article when there is no longer enough room for a useful side-by-side relationship.
  • Do not shrink prose to preserve a desktop two-column arrangement.
  • Preserve source order through reflow; responsive CSS may change geometry without changing meaning.
  • Allow navigation and related links to wrap naturally.
  • Prevent long content, media, or generated strings from forcing horizontal page overflow.

Application & content-platform ownership

  • Routes and destination URLs remain application-owned.
  • Content records, revision history, publishing status, permissions, localization source, and persistence remain application- or CMS-owned.
  • Search indexing, analytics, personalization, recommendations, and content ranking remain product concerns.
  • Concord defines the page-scale composition and the contracts used by participating Layout, Components, and Patterns; it does not implement a content platform.
  • Products may add domain-specific metadata or actions while preserving the Template's semantic hierarchy and reading model.

Theme, density & page-scale validation

Validate the same semantic article under supported themes, light and dark appearance, density settings, text zoom, narrow available widths, and long localized content.

Theme may change color and surface expression. Density may tune spacing around supporting controls and regions. Neither should change heading order, source order, link purpose, readable content hierarchy, landmark meaning, or application ownership.
